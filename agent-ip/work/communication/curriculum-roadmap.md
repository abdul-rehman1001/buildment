# Curriculum roadmap: Communication & Articulating Ideas

## In one paragraph

Fellows in Gaza who are building tech careers practice the communication habits that remote teams and freelance clients judge them on. They learn to put the point first, explain a technical idea to someone outside tech, listen and ask clarifying questions, plan a short talk or project demo, answer questions calmly, and tell a short true work story. Every module ends with real evidence from the fellow's own work: a rewritten message, an explanation, a listening reflection, a talk outline and a work story. The course ends with a five-minute talk or demo about the fellow's own work, submitted as a video, an audio recording or a full written script, and reviewed by a mentor for clarity, not for accent or English level. The chapters already exist; this roadmap describes the draft so instructors can approve, revise or reject its shape.

## Learner and entry prerequisites

- Learner: fellows in Gaza starting or growing tech careers (junior developers, testers, freelancers, students). English is a second language. Most learn on phones, with unreliable power and internet.
- Prior knowledge: can read short, plain English texts and write a few sentences (voice input is available in every open question). Has at least one project from work, study, freelancing or personal practice to talk about; modules 2, 4 and 6 depend on it. No public speaking experience needed.
- Time available: about 9 hours on the platform plus 1 to 2 hours of practice with other people, over 2 weeks at 2 sessions a week. Allow a 3-week window for power and internet cuts.
- Entry diagnostic: none built. The open question in chapter 1.1 (a real message that got a confused reply) works as an informal baseline a mentor can read.

## Course shape

Part-time pace: 2 sessions a week of about 2 to 3 hours, over 2 weeks. Hours below are summed from chapter `estimatedMinutes` and do not include off-platform practice (trying an explanation on a relative, practice runs, recording and uploading).

| Session | Focus | Modules | Hours | Evidence at the end |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (week 1) | Point first, explain to a non-technical listener | 1, 2 | 2.7 | Rewritten message; explanation of a technical idea |
| 2 (week 1) | Listening and clarifying questions, with a live group discussion | 3 | 1.7 | Listening reflection after the discussion |
| 3 (week 2) | Talks, demos and stories | 4, 5 | 2.75 | Talk outline; one work story |
| 4 (week 2) | Capstone: prepare, practice, submit | 6 | 1.7 | Five-minute talk (recording or script) |

`course.yaml` lists 10 hours. The 1.25-hour gap is roughly the off-platform practice, so 10 hours is a fair public number.

## Gate evidence at a glance

| Module | Gate evidence |
|---|---|
| 1 | Checklist, learning log, and a written rewrite of one of the fellow's own messages (60 words minimum) |
| 2 | Checklist, learning log, and an explanation of a technical idea for a named non-technical listener, written or as a recording link (tried on a real person first) |
| 3 | Checklist, learning log, participation in a 30-minute facilitated group discussion (or a 15-minute partner or written-chat fallback), and a 100-word listening reflection |
| 4 | Checklist, learning log, and a 120-word talk outline with backup plan and one expected question; optional practice recording link |
| 5 | Checklist, learning log, and one 80 to 120 word true work story |
| 6 | Checklist and learning log; the capstone itself is submitted in 6.3 |

A mentor reads every gate submission. Quizzes are graded automatically and explain every option.

## Capstone

- What the learner submits: a five-minute talk or demo about their own work or study, as a video link, an audio-only link, or the full written script (about 600 to 750 words, with square-bracket notes for what is on screen in a demo). All three are accepted equally.
- Mentor review: the mentor checks clarity against the list in 6.2: main point in one sentence and within the first 30 seconds, words that fit the listener, an opening plus three parts plus an ending, one example or story, about five minutes, an ending that says what happens next, and no private data. The mentor does not grade accent, English level or video quality. The draft promises "feedback and questions, not a score."
- Defense or follow-up questions: the mentor replies with questions, but the draft has no step where the learner answers them, so the question-handling skill from 4.4 is never observed. See open decision 7.
- Submission requirements:
  - The talk is about the fellow's own real work, study or project.
  - The main point is stated early and repeated at the end.
  - A recording link opens without sign-in (tested in a private window).
  - A written script contains the full spoken words, not headings.
  - Test data only; no client names, prices, passwords or customer data.

## Variants

None planned. Soft-skills track; see open decisions.

## Out of scope

- Workplace English, emails, standups, stakeholder management, networking and full interview preparation. These belong to the Professional & Business Communication course; this course only teaches the story shape that interviews reuse.
- Giving and receiving feedback, conflict and negotiation. These belong to the Collaboration, Feedback & Conflict Resolution course.
- Slide design, video editing and presentation software. Fellows on phones with weak connections should not need them, and clarity does not depend on them.
- Pronunciation, accent and voice coaching. The course deliberately judges clarity, not accent, so fellows are not penalized for their English level.
- Long-form writing such as specs, proposals and documentation, and speaking to large audiences. Both need more time than a 10-hour course allows.
