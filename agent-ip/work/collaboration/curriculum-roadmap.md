# Curriculum roadmap: Collaboration, Feedback & Conflict Resolution

## In one paragraph

A short soft-skills course for fellows in Gaza who are building tech careers on remote teams, freelance projects and NGO work. Each module opens with a realistic case (a remote team, a client, an NGO volunteer group), teaches one simple pattern, and ends with a piece of writing about the learner's own situation. The learner practices agreeing roles and handoffs, giving specific feedback (situation, behavior, impact), receiving criticism without becoming defensive, disagreeing about ideas without damaging the relationship, and negotiating scope, time and cost. The course ends with a live, scripted role-play with two other fellows or a mentor, followed by a written reflection that a mentor reads and answers. At the end the learner can plan and hold a hard team conversation and say specifically what they will do differently next time.

This roadmap describes the course as it is already drafted in `content/import/collaboration/`. The chapters were written before this gate; see open decision 1.

## Learner and entry prerequisites

- **Learner:** Fellows in Gaza who are starting or growing tech careers (developers, designers, freelancers, NGO volunteers). English is a second language. Most learn on phones, with unreliable power and internet.
- **Prior knowledge:** None required. The learner should be able to read short plain-English paragraphs and write a few sentences. Some experience of a team (work, study, a project or volunteering) helps, but every written task offers a practice scenario for learners who have none.
- **Time available:** Part-time. About 9 hours in total, done as four sessions of 2 to 3 hours over 2 to 3 weeks, plus one live one-hour role-play session that must be scheduled with other people.
- **Entry diagnostic:** None in the draft. The first open question in chapter 1.1 (describe a team that worked or did not) acts as an informal baseline the mentor can compare with the final reflection.

## Capstone

- **What the learner submits:** A written reflection (chapter 6.3) on a live role-play they took part in, in five parts: what happened, what worked, what was hard, what the observer saw, what they will change. It names the script and the role played. A recording link is optional.
- **Live session (in place of a five-minute demo):** About one hour on a call with two other fellows. Three rounds of 3 minutes preparation, 10 minutes conversation and 7 minutes debrief, rotating role A, role B and observer. Script A is a feedback conversation about missed handoffs; Script B is a scope and deadline conflict with a client. If the learner cannot find two fellows, a mentor plays role B and the observer questions are answered together afterward.
- **How a mentor reviews it:** The mentor reads the written reflection and replies with questions and suggestions, not a score. The draft tells learners the mentor looks for specific moments with real words, an honest look at what was hard, one clear change, and a real situation where the learner will use it.
- **Follow-up questions (proposed for mentors; not yet in the draft):** What exact words did you use at the hardest moment, and what would you say instead? What did the observer see that you did not? When did you ask your first real question, and what changed after it? Where and when will you use your change in the next two weeks?
- **Submission requirements:** At least 150 words; all five parts; script and role named; written within about a day of the session; about a session that really happened (the draft tells learners not to write about a session that did not take place and to contact their mentor instead); recordings only with everyone's consent, shared only with the mentor, openable without signing in.

## Variants

None planned. Soft-skills track; see open decisions.

## Out of scope

- **Formal HR, legal or disciplinary processes.** Chapter 4.4 tells learners when to escalate and to keep messages and dates, but does not teach how complaints are investigated. That belongs to the fellowship team and employers.
- **Counselling, trauma or mental-health support.** The course acknowledges hard days and power cuts but does not try to support distress. Mentors should refer on (see open decision 3).
- **Setting prices or rates.** Chapter 5.3 covers how to talk when a client questions a rate, not what to charge. Pricing is a business topic, not a collaboration skill.
- **Salary and job-offer negotiation.** Different stakes and norms; better placed in an interviews or career course.
- **Managing or leading a team.** Performance reviews, hiring and team leadership are beyond a beginner course; the learner practices as a peer, freelancer or volunteer.
- **Writing style, emails, meetings and presentations.** Covered in the sibling Professional & Business Communication and Communication courses.
- **Collaboration tools** (Jira, Slack, GitHub settings). The patterns work in any tool, including WhatsApp, which most fellows already use.
