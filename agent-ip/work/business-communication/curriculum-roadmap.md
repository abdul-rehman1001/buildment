# Curriculum roadmap: Professional & Business Communication

This roadmap describes the course as it was drafted (commit 89eb05c, `content/import/business-communication/`). The chapters were written before this gate. Instructors are asked to confirm the shape, not the prose.

## In one paragraph

Fellows in Gaza who are starting tech careers with international clients and teams learn the written habits of professional remote work: clear and polite English, emails that get answered, standup and status updates, managing client expectations, delivering bad news, running short meetings, reaching out on LinkedIn and answering interview questions. Every module ends with a real message the learner writes about their own work. The course ends with a communication pack for one real or realistic project (a kickoff email, a weekly status update, a bad-news message and an interview answer that tell one consistent story) which a mentor reads and answers like a client or team lead would.

## Learner and entry prerequisites

- **Learner:** a Buildment fellow in Gaza, junior developer or student, starting freelance work or applying for remote roles. English is a second language. Most study on a phone, with power cuts and weak internet.
- **Prior knowledge:** can read and write simple English sentences; has done at least one project (study, volunteer, freelance or personal) they can describe. No business or client experience assumed.
- **Time available:** part-time, about 2 to 3 hours per session, two or three sessions a week, alongside study or work.
- **Entry diagnostic:** none formal. Chapter 1.1 asks the learner to paste a real message they sent and say what they were unsure about. That message becomes the "before" for the module 1 gate rewrite.

## Capstone

- **What the learner submits:** one communication pack for a real or realistic project, built from a five-line fact sheet: (1) kickoff email, (2) weekly status update, (3) bad-news message about the problem named in 7.1, (4) a STAR answer to "Tell me about a time a project did not go as planned" telling the same story. A first line states whether the project is real or realistic.
- **How a mentor reviews it:** reads the pack as a client or team lead would, against the per-piece checklists in 7.2 (each row cites the chapter that taught it). Checks that each piece is clear, polite and complete, and that names, dates and scope match across all four. Feedback is written comments and questions, not a score.
- **Defense or follow-up questions:** the draft says the mentor replies "with feedback and questions" but lists none. Proposed set for mentors: Why did you recommend that option over the others? Which promise in your kickoff email did the bad-news message have to account for? How would piece 3 change if the reader were the designer, not the client? What follow-up would an interviewer ask about your story, and what is your answer? Which line would you cut so the update reads in 30 seconds?
- **Submission requirements:** one open question, minimum 300 words (most packs 350 to 600); four pieces under headings, with targets of 100 to 180, 60 to 120, 120 to 200 and 150 to 250 words; no passwords, private client data, secret prices or real email addresses; interview recording link optional, the written pack is what is reviewed. Writing can be drafted offline on a phone or paper during power cuts.

## Variants

None planned. Soft-skills track; see open decisions.

## Out of scope

- **General English grammar, pronunciation and speaking fluency.** This is not an ESL course. It teaches the patterns of work messages; language support stays with mentors and other programs.
- **Presentations, demos, structured explaining and active listening.** Taught in Communication & Articulating Ideas.
- **Giving and receiving feedback, conflict and negotiation.** Taught in Collaboration, Feedback & Conflict Resolution. This course only touches new requests through "answer with options".
- **CVs, cover letters, job search strategy and freelance platform profiles (Upwork, Fiverr).** Large topics of their own; the course covers only LinkedIn headline, outreach and interview answers.
- **Proposals, contracts, pricing, invoicing and getting paid across borders.** Need legal and financial context specific to Gaza that the course cannot verify; 6.4 only says pay questions are fair to ask.
- **Live mock interviews and spoken assessment.** Not required, because of bandwidth and mentor time; recordings are optional everywhere except where open decision 4 changes that.
