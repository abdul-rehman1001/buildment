# Curriculum roadmap: Ownership, Initiative & Adaptability

## In one paragraph
A short soft-skills course for fellows in Gaza who are building tech careers. Learners practice six work habits that make remote people trusted: owning the outcome and their mistakes, spotting gaps and proposing fixes, turning unclear assignments into clear goals, finishing work all the way, learning new tools alone, and recovering calmly when plans change or go wrong. Every module ends with a piece of their own real work, not a hypothetical: a mistake report, a proposal for a real gap, a breakdown of an unclear task, the first finished step of an unfinished task, a learning plan and a setback reflection. The capstone is a personal improvement plan for the next six to eight weeks that a mentor reviews and the learner starts before finishing the course.

## Learner and entry prerequisites
- Learner: a fellow in Gaza who is working, freelancing, studying or volunteering toward a tech career. English is a second language. Most study on a phone, with power and internet that can drop for hours or days.
- Prior knowledge: none on the topic. Learners need to read short plain-English paragraphs (every business term is explained, and answers can be typed or spoken through voice input). They need at least one current or recent task from work, study or a volunteer project to draw on. Scenarios use software terms such as ticket, pull request and deploy, so some exposure to tech work helps.
- Time available: about 2 to 3 hours a week in two or three short sittings, over four weeks, plus a few real-world actions between sittings.
- Entry diagnostic: none in the draft. The open question in chapter 1.1 (a task you finished recently: did you check it worked for the person who needed it?) acts as a baseline that the module 1 learning log returns to.

## Gate evidence at a glance
Every gate has a self-ticked checklist (4 to 6 boxes) and a learning log of 4 or 5 reflection questions.

| Module | Gate evidence |
|---|---|
| 1 | Checklist and log only; the written evidence is the 1.3 mistake report and the 1.4 quiz |
| 2 | Five-part proposal for a real gap (100+ words, mentor reviewed); box for "sent it, or chose a day to send it" |
| 3 | Breakdown of a vague assignment: goal, success, audience, 3 to 5 questions, 3+ assumptions, start or wait (150+ words, mentor reviewed) |
| 4 | Report on finishing the first step of a real task, with steps and next date (80+ words, mentor reviewed) |
| 5 | Learning plan (80+ words); boxes for docs saved offline, a notes file and a help question; no reviewer named |
| 6 | Reflection on a change or setback (80+ words, mentor reads; learner may pick a small example) |
| 7 | Boxes: plan submitted in 7.2, first habit done, review date set, check-in person told |

## Capstone
- What the learner submits: a one-page improvement plan (200+ words) in chapter 7.2 with five parts: two or three goals with "done" and a date, one habit per goal with a minimum version for days without power or internet, if-then plans for expected obstacles (including restarting after a missed week), review dates every two weeks plus a final one, and a named check-in person and channel. An audio or video explanation link is optional.
- How a mentor reviews it: for realism, not ambition, using the five checks in "Before you submit" (goals someone else can verify, minimum versions, honest obstacles, real dates, a named person). The draft promises questions, not a score.
- Defense or follow-up questions: written replies from the mentor, for example "What happens to this habit on a day with no power?" and "How will you know this goal is done?". On the first review date the learner sends a progress check (habit days, a win, what got in the way, one change).
- Submission requirements: all five parts present; minimum word count met; at least one habit started; first review date recorded; no private family or health details required.

## Variants
None planned. Soft-skills track; see open decisions.

## Out of scope
- Technical skills themselves. Module 5 teaches how to learn a tool, not any specific tool.
- Prioritization, trade-offs and deciding under risk: taught in Decision-Making & Professional Judgment.
- Giving and receiving feedback, conflict and negotiation: taught in Collaboration, Feedback & Conflict Resolution.
- Workplace English, emails, stakeholder updates and interviews: taught in Professional & Business Communication.
- Full productivity systems. Only single ideas are borrowed (next action, if-then plans), to keep the course short.
- Mental health care. Module 6 signposts support and does not counsel.
- Career planning and job search beyond the improvement plan.
